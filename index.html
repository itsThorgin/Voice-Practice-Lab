The detected note is the nearest note to your voice. When a target is selected, cents and direction compare your pitch with that target. Hold Play reference to listen; release to stop.

Frequency (Hz): How many times the detected sound wave repeats each second. A higher number means a higher pitch.

Cents: How far your pitch is above or below the target, or the nearest note when no target is selected. 100 cents is one semitone. Negative means low; positive means high.

Confidence: How clearly the detector finds a repeating sound pattern. A higher percentage means a clearer pitch estimate, not a better voice or a guaranteed correct note.

Stability: How much your recent pitch varies, in cents. A smaller number means a steadier pitch. It does not show how close you are to the target.

Voice state: Whether the app detects a reliable pitch. Noise, quiet sound or speech without a clear pitch can show No pitch detected.

Held duration (s): How long your detected pitch stays within 20 cents of the selected target. It resets when your pitch leaves that range, detection becomes unreliable or the target changes. No target means no timer. Reference playback resets this measurement. Guided practice has its own hold goals.

About very low and high notes

This app can analyze a wide pitch range, but its accuracy depends on your device. Your microphone captures your voice, while your headphones or speakers reproduce reference tones. Some devices filter out very low frequencies or cannot capture and reproduce very high frequencies accurately.

At extreme frequencies, the app may be less precise or show No reliable pitch detected. This does not necessarily mean you produced the note incorrectly; it means the app cannot measure it reliably with the current browser and hardware.

      Understand your score

      Your practice score is out of 100 points:

      • Accuracy · 50 points: singing close to the target.
      • Stability · 20 points: keeping your pitch consistent.
      • Hold · 20 points: maintaining a continuous match.
      • Completion · 10 points: completing the note.

      A 0.5-second hold earns 10 hold points. A 1-second hold earns all 20. Separate holds do not add together.

      The score includes readings from the whole attempt. Completing the note does not guarantee 100 points.

      Keep in mind

      • Release the reference before singing. Playing it breaks your hold while the attempt timer continues.
      • Too few reliable pitch readings means no score, rather than zero.
      • Retry starts a fresh attempt. Results disappear when you reload or leave the page.
      Score details

      Compare attempts with similar goals, settings and equipment. This score measures pitch practice, not voice quality or musical expression.

      How the points are calculated

      Accuracy: find the average distance from the target, in cents. Errors above and below count equally. Subtract this average from 100, then divide by 2, for up to 50 points. An average error of 20 cents earns 40 points. The separate Pitch accuracy score shows closeness out of 100 before this division.

      Stability: find the middle pitch reading, then the middle distance of the readings from it. This is the stability spread (MAD). Start with 20 points and subtract 0.4 points per cent of spread. A spread of 5 cents earns 18 points. A steady note can still be off target. Vibrato can reduce stability points.

      Hold: divide the best qualifying hold by the required duration, then multiply by 20. The maximum is 20 points. Use the goal for the selected mode; a 1-second match and a 5-second sustained hold have different requirements.

      Completion: earn 10 points when the app confirms that this note's goal is complete. An unfinished or skipped note earns no completion points but keeps its other measured points.

      No component can be below zero. Expressed as percentages, the formula is 50% accuracy + 20% stability + 20% best hold + 10% completion. The app adds unrounded values, then rounds the total to a whole number. Point contributions are displayed to one decimal place.

      Read the measurements

      • Mean absolute error: average distance from the target. Lower is better.
      • Median absolute error: the middle distance when errors are sorted.
      • Mean signed error: average direction of error. Negative means low; positive means high. Opposite errors can cancel here.
      • Stability spread (MAD): typical pitch variation across the attempt. Lower means steadier.
      • Voiced time: time between reliable readings no more than 120 milliseconds apart.
      • Best hold: the longest qualifying continuous match.
      • Accepted frames: the number of reliable pitch readings used.

      Missing readings and session averages

      An overall score needs at least 3 accepted readings and a stability measurement. Silence, uncertain readings and reference playback add no measurements. Brief gaps of up to 120 milliseconds may bridge a hold under the exercise rules. Longer gaps break it. A new qualifying reading is required to complete a hold.

      Random-note and scale averages give each retained result with enough data equal weight. Partial or skipped results count if they have enough data. Results without enough data are excluded and counted; scale notes you have not attempted are not averaged. Interval root and target stages have separate scores.

      How this attempt is measured

      A steady match needs at least 3 reliable readings, a recent stability spread of no more than 10 cents, and pitch within ±20 cents for 1 second. The recent spread uses up to 1 second of readings; the stability score uses the whole attempt.

      Reference playback pauses measurements until 250 milliseconds after release. The attempt timer continues. Use headphones to reduce speaker pickup.

      Retry replaces the displayed attempt. In Random notes, the last 100 ended attempts stay in this session, including retries and stopped attempts. Reset random session, changing mode, or leaving the page clears them. Reset attempt clears the selected-target attempt.

      How a sustained hold is measured

      Stay inside the allowed error for your chosen duration. A reliable reading outside this range breaks the hold immediately. Completion depends on the hold, not the stability label.

      Recent stability uses up to 1 second of readings and needs at least 3 readings. A spread of no more than 10 cents is labelled steady. The stability score uses the whole attempt.

      Progress freezes when reliable pitch is missing. A gap of up to 120 milliseconds can be bridged when matching pitch returns. A longer gap resets the current hold but keeps your best hold. Measured time inside tolerance counts only adjacent matching readings; gaps add no measured time. Each period of missing or unreliable pitch counts as one detector dropout.

      Reference playback breaks the hold and pauses measurements until 250 milliseconds after release. It does not count as a detector dropout. The 30-second attempt timer continues. Use headphones to reduce speaker pickup.

      Changing the hold settings, target, tuning or range ends the attempt. Its result keeps the original goal. Retry starts fresh. Reset sustained session clears results and restores the 5-second, ±20-cent defaults. Changing mode or leaving the page clears the session.

      Find your pace

      Metronome

      Music mode gives you an exact beat. BPM means beats per minute. A group such as 3 + 2 puts a strong beat at the start of each group. Turn off Strong beats for equal beats. Subdivisions add quieter pulses between beats. For 6/8 and other compound patterns, BPM counts each small beat.

      In Speech pacing, one pulse represents one word at your target average pace. At 155 WPM, you hear or see 155 pulses per minute. Use this as a rhythm guide. Keep your natural pauses and expression; you do not need to land every word on a pulse. Choose a delivery style and Relaxed, Typical, or Brisk. Presets are starting points, not measured WPM or required delivery speeds.

      Sound and visual cues work independently. A salmon edge and outer glow rise and fade with every main beat. Pulse other practice panels applies it to all collapsible panels, including Settings. Intensity adjusts how much the glow rises on each beat. Reduced motion keeps the beat label without the glow and follows your device preference. These controls stay in this session only. Leaving the page stops playback; return and press Start to play again.

      Set the background

      Noise generator

      Choose a noise and press Start. One noise plays at a time. Switching tabs while playing changes the sound. Each noise remembers its volume for this session. Noise can play during exercises and recording. It stops when you leave the page; press Start to play again.

      Noise is generated continuously on your device using fresh random samples. There is no repeating recording or loop join. The metronome sounds are also generated locally.

      White noise: roughly equal energy in equal-width frequency bands. It sounds bright and can make other background sounds less noticeable. Start at the default 0.50% and raise it only as needed.

      Pink noise: roughly equal energy in each octave, with less high-frequency energy than white noise. Try it when white noise sounds too sharp. It starts at 0.50%.

      Brown noise: more low-frequency energy and a deeper sound than pink noise. Try it when you prefer a darker background. It starts at 2.00%. Small phone speakers may reproduce little of the low rumble.

      These sounds provide a background for practice. They do not remove noise from your recording. Your microphone may pick them up; use headphones or lower the volume if this affects your readings or recordings.
